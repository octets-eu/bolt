Bolt hide and seek: wireframe

Eight screens on a tablet held sideways, in the order a child meets them. Boxes and words only: no colours, no final shapes. A dark button is the one thing to tap next. Assumptions that need a yes or no are at the bottom.

1Bolts

The app opens here. Bolts it knows connect on their own; each shows its sign on its matrix, the same sign as on its card.

  • “Find a Bolt” opens the Bluetooth picker for a new one.
  • “Next” turns dark when two Bolts are awake.
  • The bar under a Bolt is its battery.

2Rooms

One card per room the Bolts know, shown as its cloud of bump points and a picture the child chose.

  • A tap picks a room (thick frame), “Next” goes on.
  • “New room” starts the exploring.
  • No typing: a room gets a picture, a name is optional.

3New room

Both Bolts roam. Every bump leaves a dot, and the dots slowly draw the walls and the furniture.

  • The ring is the exploration indicator: it grows while they roam. At 100 % the Bolts stop on their own and the ring becomes the dark “Next ▶”.
  • Then the child picks a picture for the room. Stop ends the exploring early; the room is kept as far as it got.
  • The round button stops both Bolts at once, on every screen where they move.

4Turn the map

The map has to lie like the room. One Bolt rolls a short way straight ahead on the floor; the map shows the same roll as an arrow.

  • The child drags the dark knob round the ring, and the map turns with it, until the arrow points the way the Bolt rolled.
  • “Roll again” repeats the roll, as often as needed.
  • “It fits” confirms. From then on the map stays right when the child turns with the tablet.

5Get ready

The room with both Bolts on it, and the two roles. This is also where a Bolt is put in its place.

  • The arrows swap seeker and hider.
  • The round arrow goes back to “Turn the map”, should the map lie wrong.
  • A Bolt carried by hand is dragged to its new spot on the map.

6Hiding

The hider rolls away to a spot of its own. The seeker stays, its matrix shows closed eyes and counts.

  • When the hider stands still it starts calling, and the seeker sets off.
  • Nothing to tap but stop: the child watches the floor.

7Seeking

Both Bolts move on the map as they move on the floor. The seeker draws its path; a small arrow shows where it believes the hider is.

  • Rings around the hider show it is calling.
  • Time is a bar that fills, not a number.
  • The map fills the screen; title, time and stop float on top of it.
  • The map turns with the tablet; it is not turned by hand here.

8Found

The seeker bumped into the hider and both felt it. A sound plays, on the tablet and on the Bolts.

  • Stars for how quick it was, never a fail screen.
  • “Again” plays the same roles, “Swap” the other way round, “End” goes back to the rooms.
  • If the seeker gives up, the same screen says “Not found this time” with the same three buttons.

Built for six and up

  • One dark button per screen: the next thing to do.
  • The map is the screen. It is always shown whole, never cut off at an edge, also when it turns; buttons float over it in the corners.
  • Every button is at least a thumb wide; nothing sits in a menu.
  • Few words, short ones; a picture or a sign wherever one will do. No typing anywhere.
  • A Bolt on the screen and the Bolt on the floor carry the same sign, so the child knows which is which.
  • Stop is always in the same corner and stops both Bolts.
  • Nothing can be broken by a wrong tap: every step can be gone back from, and no screen says “wrong”.

Assumed, to confirm

  • Device: a tablet or phone held sideways, touch. Web Bluetooth needs Chrome on Android or a computer; it does not run on iPad or iPhone.
  • Who drives away: the hider (the sender) rolls off and hides; the seeker waits. The request said “seeker drives away”.
  • Map in sync: the child turns the map once by dragging and confirms; after that the tablet's own sensors keep it lying like the room.
  • The cue for turning: a Bolt rolls straight ahead and the map shows that roll as an arrow. Without a cue a child cannot tell when a cloud of dots fits.
  • Words: “hider” for the sender; Bolts get a sign and a short name instead of “SB-9129”.
  • Placing by hand: the child carries a Bolt and drags its picture to the new spot on the map.

Not drawn yet

  • A Bolt that is lost, stuck or low on battery in the middle of a game.
  • Only one Bolt awake.
  • Choosing the room's picture after exploring.
  • What 100 % explored means: the rule that decides the room is known well enough is not defined yet.
  • Anything for a grown-up: forgetting a Bolt, deleting a room.
